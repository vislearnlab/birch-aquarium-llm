Leopard Sharks are named for their unique saddles and spot pattern.

Most Leopard Sharks have 13 saddle patches across their back!

Leopard Sharks are a local species in San Diego's waters and are often seen within view of Birch Aquarium.

Leopard Sharks are found from Oregon down to Baja California and into the Gulf of California, Mexico.

They are often seen cruising or hunting in coastal rocky reefs, seagrass beds, kelp forests, and along the sandy shore.

Leopard Sharks have a mouth the size of an adult human's mouth that is located on the bottom of their head.

Scientists did not know why female Leopard Sharks gathered close to shore at La Jolla Shores Beach. Scripps Institution of Oceanography researchers discovered the aggregation was made up of pregnant female sharks. The mothers were incubating their unborn pups in the warm, calm, shallow waters.

Unlike most fish, which lay eggs, a mother leopard shark keeps her eggs inside her body until they hatch. After 10 to 12 months, she gives birth to a litter of 7 to 36 pups.

It is still a mystery where La Jolla's Leopard Sharks go to give birth.
