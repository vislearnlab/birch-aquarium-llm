Seahorses, pipefish, and seadragons are in the family Syngnathidae.

The name "seahorse" is derived from ancient Greek and means "fused jaw."

Seahorses do not have teeth. Instead they suck small prey in through their trap-door like mouth. If it's very quiet you can hear a seahorse snap up its food!

Despite their unusual look, seahorses are a type of fish! There are nearly 40 types of seahorses, and the number changes as scientists discover more species or study their genetics.

Seahorses are usually found in coastal temperate and tropical seas.

Seahorses are not strong swimmers, but their prehensile tails are great for gripping onto things.

Many seahorse species are endangered or threatened because of habitat loss, collection for the aquarium trade and the traditional medicine trade, climate change, and pollution.

Seahorse breeding programs at aquariums work to relieve pressure on wild seahorse populations by captively breeding seahorses.
