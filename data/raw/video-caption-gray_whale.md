Adult gray whales are about the size of a school bus — 45 feet!

Eastern gray whales make one of the longest migrations of any mammal. Each year they journey more than 10,000 miles.

Gray whales feed in the Arctic, and mate and give birth in lagoons in Mexico.

Gray whales are covered in barnacles and whale lice — an adult gray whale can carry around 500 pounds of these creatures living on them.

Gray whale calves weigh about 2,000 pounds at birth — about the same size as a small car!

Gray whales communicate using clicks and grunts. They are usually silent when migrating to avoid their main predator, orcas.

Eastern gray whales are a conservation success story: they have bounced back from near-extinction twice in the last 100 years, and now there are more than 25,000 of them.
