Clownfish are also called anemonefish because of their unique homes.

There are 30 known species of clownfish, and more will likely be discovered.

All clownfish are found in the shallow tropical waters of the Pacific Ocean, Indian Ocean, and Red Sea.

Clownfish are most famous for their relationship with sea anemones. Clownfish perform an elaborate dance with an anemone before taking up residence, gently touching the anemone's stinging tentacles with different parts of their bodies until they are acclimated to their host.

A thick layer of mucus on a clownfish's skin protects it from the anemone's sting.

The clownfish-anemone relationship is symbiotic — it benefits both animals. The clownfish receives safety from predators and food scraps, in exchange for driving off intruders and removing parasites from the anemone.

All clownfish are born male. They will switch their sex to female, but only to become the dominant female of a group.
