Lionfish are native to Indo-Pacific tropical habitats like coral and rocky reefs. They are unfortunately now an invasive species worldwide.

Invasive lionfish compete with native species for food and space. This has caused problems in places where they were accidentally introduced, such as in the Caribbean and U.S. waters from Florida to North Carolina.

Female lionfish can lay over 2 million eggs in a year and reproduce all year long. This, combined with having no natural predators, has made them a challenging invasive species to manage.

Lionfish are voracious predators with lightning fast reflexes. They sometimes use their large fins to corner prey against the reef.

Lionfish have 18 long venomous spines along their dorsal fin that they use as self defense.

A lionfish can grow to be longer than a foot and live for more than 15 years.
