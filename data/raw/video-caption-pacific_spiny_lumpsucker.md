Pacific spiny lumpsuckers are small fish with big personalities. They are round, ping-pong-ball-shaped fish with specialized suction-cup-like fins.

A Pacific spiny lumpsucker's pelvic fin is a modified suction disk, which it uses to attach itself to rocks or seaweed.

Their scientific family name, Cyclopteridae, comes from the Greek words "kyklos" meaning circle, and "pteryx" meaning wing or fin — referring to this specialized fin.

Pacific spiny lumpsuckers do not have scales. Instead the body is covered with plate-like structures with spiny lumps called tubercles.

They are found in cold seaweed and rocky-bottomed communities of the Pacific Northwest, up to Alaska, and Japan.

Baby Pacific spiny lumpsuckers are able to stick to surfaces with their specialized fins just like their parents.

Because of their round shape, Pacific spiny lumpsuckers are not strong swimmers. They rely on camouflage to protect themselves.
