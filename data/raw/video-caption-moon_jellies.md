# Moon Jellies — birch-ask-v2 video captions

source: video-caption:moon_jellies

Moon jellies are named for their ethereal, moon-like bell.

A moon jelly's bell can grow up to 24 inches across.

Moon jellies travel with the currents — technically they are a type of plankton.

Their favorite food is tiny zooplankton.

Moon jellies' short tentacles sweep food towards their oral arms, the frilly part on the underside of the jelly.

Moon jellies are an important food source for many ocean animals, especially sea turtles. Unfortunately, sometimes animals mistake floating plastic bags for moon jellies.

Moon jellies haven't been to the moon, but they have gone to space! About 2,500 young moon jellies went into orbit on the space shuttle Columbia in 1991.
